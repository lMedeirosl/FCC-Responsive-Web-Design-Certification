Alan Turing

The mathematician who helped shape the modern computer

A pioneer of computer science

Alan Mathison Turing was a British mathematician, logician, and computer scientist. His theoretical work laid important foundations for modern computing and artificial intelligence.

His life and achievements

  • 1912: Born in London, England, on June 23.
  • 1936: Published a groundbreaking paper introducing the theoretical model now known as the Turing machine.
  • 1939–1945: Contributed to the Allied effort during World War II by helping decipher German encrypted communications at Bletchley Park.
  • 1950: Published a paper exploring machine intelligence and proposing what became known as the Turing test.
  • 1952: Published research on mathematical biology, including the chemical basis of morphogenesis.
  • 1954: Died in Wilmslow, England, at the age of 41.
  • 2013: Received a posthumous royal pardon from Queen Elizabeth II.

A lasting legacy

Turing's contributions extended beyond mathematics. His work on computation helped establish the theoretical foundations of computer science, while his wartime contributions played a significant role in the history of cryptanalysis.

Today, he is remembered as one of the most influential figures in the development of computing and the study of artificial intelligence.
